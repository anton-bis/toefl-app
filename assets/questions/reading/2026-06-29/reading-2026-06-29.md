### Task 4 Read an Academic Passage – The Shifting Art of Playwriting (Questions 16–20)

Innovative playwriting techniques have transformed the theatrical landscape by challenging linear storytelling and passive spectatorship. Nonlinear narratives, seen in plays like Harold Pinter's Betrayal, disrupt chronological order to reflect the fragmented nature of memory and emotion. This structure invites audiences to actively reconstruct the story, deepening their engagement.

Another innovation is breaking the fourth wall, where characters directly address the audience. Used by Bertolt Brecht in Mother Courage and Her Children, this technique encourages viewers to reflect critically rather than become absorbed in illusion. Fully interactive theater, exemplified by Sleep No More, goes further by allowing viewers to move through the performance space.

### Task 5 Read an Academic Passage – Microseisms: The Earth's Subtle Vibrations (Questions 21–25)

The Earth is constantly vibrating, even when there are no earthquakes. These subtle vibrations, known as microseisms, are typically caused by ocean waves. When waves collide, pressure variations travel through the water and into the seafloor, generating seismic waves detectable by seismometers worldwide.

Researchers use microseisms to study the Earth's interior, reconstruct past ocean conditions, and identify potential drilling sites. The signals are mild and easily masked by other seismic noises, so advanced filtering methods and data from multiple seismometers are essential.

### Task 6 Read an Academic Passage – Polarized Light's Surprising Applications (Questions 26–30)

Polarized light, characterized by waves vibrating in a single plane, has applications beyond sunglasses. It reduces glare and can reveal hidden properties of crystals, cells, and tissues. In biology, polarized light helps researchers examine cellular organization and disease mechanisms.
