### Task 5 Read an Academic Passage – The Shifting Art of Playwriting (Questions 26–30)

Innovative playwriting techniques have transformed the theatrical landscape by challenging linear storytelling and passive spectatorship. Nonlinear narratives, seen in plays like Harold Pinter's Betrayal, disrupt chronological order to reflect the fragmented nature of memory and emotion. This structure invites audiences to actively reconstruct the story, deepening their engagement.

Another innovation is the so-called breaking of the fourth wall, where characters directly address the audience. Used by Bertolt Brecht in Mother Courage and Her Children, this technique encourages viewers to reflect critically rather than become absorbed in illusion. Fully interactive theater, exemplified by the British production Sleep No More, goes further by allowing viewers to physically move through the performance space, personalizing the experience and making each viewing unique.

These techniques suggest a broader hypothesis: that theatrical form is not merely a container for content but a vital part of creating meaning. By reshaping narrative structure and audience dynamics, playwrights can evoke new emotional and intellectual responses, revealing complex aspects of the human condition.

Of course, such innovations may confuse rather than enlighten more traditional-minded theater audiences and also disrupt their emotional immersion and fragment the narrative. Thus, while these techniques can serve as powerful tools, their success depends on how carefully they are integrated into the dramatic work.

### Task 6 Read an Academic Passage – Microseisms: The Earth's Subtle Vibrations (Questions 31–35)

The Earth is constantly vibrating, even when there are no earthquakes. These subtle vibrations, known as microseisms, are typically caused by ocean waves. When waves collide, they create pressure variations that travel through the water and into the sea floor. This energy transfer generates seismic waves detectable by seismometers worldwide. Interestingly, microseisms are strongest during storms when ocean waves are more powerful.

Researchers have found these vibrations can provide important information about the Earth's interior. Microseisms travel through the Earth's crust and mantle, revealing details about the geological structures they pass through, including variations in rock density and the presence of faults or fractures. Another compelling use of microseisms is in climate studies. Scientists analyze historical data on these vibrations to reconstruct past ocean conditions and storm activities. This helps improve climate models and understand long-term weather patterns. Additionally, microseisms are used in oil and gas exploration. They identify potential drilling sites by highlighting subsurface structures without more invasive techniques.

Studying microseisms is challenging. The mild signals are easily masked by other seismic noises. Advanced data analysis techniques and state-of-the-art equipment are essential for accurately interpreting microseisms. Researchers often employ filtering methods and cross-referencing data from multiple seismometers.

### Task 2 Read an Academic Passage – Polarized Light's Surprising Applications (Questions 11–15)

Polarized light, characterized by waves vibrating in a single plane, offers applications beyond its familiar use in sunglasses, where it reduces glare from reflective surfaces like water and roads. In certain scientific fields, it is invaluable for revealing hidden properties of materials. For instance, when researchers study the internal structure of crystals, polarized light provides insights that normal light cannot, by interacting differently with the distinct optical paths within the crystal lattice.

[A] In biology, polarized light uncovers structural details of cells and tissues, facilitating breakthroughs in understanding cellular organization and disease mechanisms. This is true for birefringent materials, which alter the polarization of light in ways that reveal microstructural patterns such as fiber alignment and intracellular organization, helping scientists assess variations in cell density and composition. [B] Such techniques have been instrumental in identifying amyloid plaques in brain tissue, an indication of Alzheimer's disease. [C]

However, the use of polarized light is not universally beneficial. In aviation, pilots may avoid polarized lenses because they can obscure critical visual cues, such as instrument readings, that are essential for navigation. Thus, while polarized light can solve certain visibility issues by reducing glare, it may also interfere with necessary visual information, prompting careful consideration of context before implementation. [D]
