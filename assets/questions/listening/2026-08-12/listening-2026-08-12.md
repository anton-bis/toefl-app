### Listen to a Talk in an Ocean Science Class – Questions 5-8

subtitle: Marine Snow: Feeding the Deep and Regulating Climate
audio: listening_m1_q05_q08_lecture_marine_snow_climate.mp3

Professor: Back in the mid 1800s, scientists dredging the ocean floor stumbled on previously unknown life forms. Brittle stars, sea cucumbers, even deep sea corals. Creatures living in pitch darkness, near freezing water and under crushing pressure. That discovery flipped the old belief that such conditions couldn't support life. But what do these organisms eat? Near the ocean's surface, food chains begin with phytoplankton, tiny plants that use sunlight to turn carbon dioxide into energy. These phytoplankton feed zooplankton, which feed small fish, and these, in turn, feed larger predators. But in the deep ocean, there's no sunlight. So what keeps life going down there? The answer is something scientists call marine snow. This isn't frozen water, but a slow, steady snowfall of organic particles, dead plankton, bits of decaying creatures, and animal waste products all sinking from above. But marine snow does more than feed deep sea life. It carries carbon from surface waters down to the deep seafloor, where some of it can stay locked away for centuries, even millennia. This process helps regulate Earth's climate by pulling carbon from surface waters, and so out of the atmosphere, which interacts with the surface waters. As you probably know, carbon in the atmosphere, in the form of carbon dioxide gas, has a warming effect on Earth's climate, and the extra carbon released there from human activities has caused dangerous global warming. How much carbon does marine snow remove? Roughly 10 billion tonnes annually, about the same as all human carbon emissions combined.

### Listen to a Talk on a Podcast About Art – Questions 1-4

subtitle: Street Art: Evolution, Impact, and Challenges
audio: listening_m2_q01_q04_lecture_street_art_evolution.mp3

Professor: Today, we're going to explore the fascinating world of street art and its impact on urban environments. Street art, which includes graffiti and mural painting, has evolved from being seen as mere vandalism to being recognized as a legitimate form of artistic expression. Artists use public spaces to showcase their work, often reflecting social and political themes. One of the key benefits of street art is its ability to transform drab and neglected areas into vibrant and visually stimulating environments. Murals can brighten up a dull wall, adding color and life to a community. This transformation can foster a sense of pride among residents, encouraging them to take ownership of their surroundings. Street art can also serve as a powerful tool for social change. It provides artists with a platform to address important issues such as inequality, environmental concerns, and cultural identity. For example, artists may create pieces that highlight the struggles of marginalized communities. However, street art is not without its challenges. The legality of street art is often debated, as many artists work without official permission. This can lead to conflicts with property owners and law enforcement. Additionally, the transient nature of street art means that it can be painted over or removed at any time, making it a potentially impermanent form of expression. Despite these challenges, street art remains an influential aspect of contemporary urban culture. It allows artists to reach a wider audience and engage with the public in meaningful ways while transforming urban spaces.

### Listen to a Talk in an Astronomy Class – Questions 5-8

subtitle: Lightning in Venus's Atmosphere
audio: listening_m2_q05_q08_lecture_venus_lightning.mp3

Professor: Today, I've got a topic that might just electrify your imagination: lightning in the atmosphere of Venus. You might wonder why we should care about lightning on another planet. Well, understanding lightning on Venus could give us clues about the planet's dynamic weather systems. Okay, so the Soviet Venera space missions set out to explore Venus, one of Earth's closest neighbors. What these missions found was surprising evidence of practically the setting for a science fiction movie, but this isn't fiction. It's real science. And here's where it gets interesting. The presence of lightning suggests that the atmosphere of Venus is more dynamic than we once assumed. Why does this matter? Lightning can play a crucial role in atmospheric chemistry, affecting the formation of molecules and influencing weather patterns. On Earth, lightning is known to create compounds like nitrogen oxides, which are essential for plant life. While Venus doesn't have plant life, understanding its atmospheric processes helps scientists build models that can be applied to other planets, including our own. Moreover, studying Venusian lightning can help us predict future changes in its atmosphere. If lightning is as prevalent as the emissions suggest, it could mean that Venus is more geologically active than previously assumed. This suggests new ideas for research: could other planets with similar conditions harbor surprising phenomena, or even forms of life?
