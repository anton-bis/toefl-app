### Listen to a Talk in a music class – Questions 25-28

audio: listening-talk1.m4a
image: 19.png

Professor: Have you ever listened to a piece of music and felt like you were transported somewhere else, maybe walking through a Brazilian street market, or climbing a misty mountain trail in China? That's the magic of musical borrowing, a practice where artists blend elements from different global traditions to create something fresh and emotionally resonant. Take bossa nova, for example. It's a genre that merges the laid back swing of United States jazz music with the rhythmic pulse of Brazilian samba. When you listen to bossa nova, it feels both familiar and new at the same time. But successful musical borrowing isn't just about mixing sounds, it's about respect. When artists draw from other cultures, they need to understand the traditions behind the music. Composer Tan Dun nails this in his score for the film Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon. He blends Chinese instruments like the erhu and the pipa with western orchestration. And not just superficially. The erhu's haunting tones and the pipa's intricate rhythms reflect centuries of Chinese musical storytelling. Tan Dun doesn't just borrow, he honors the culture, letting its emotional depth shine through. Of course, fusion isn't always seamless. Sometimes the mix feels forced, or the original meaning gets lost. But when it works, when the traditions truly speak to each other, it's proof that music can be a universal language, capable of bridging worlds and telling stories that transcend borders.

### Listen to a Talk in a marine biology class – Questions 29-32

audio: listening-talk2.m4a
image: 20.png

Professor: Let's talk a little about sea otters and their importance to their own and indeed other ecosystems. We'll start with a few basic facts. The sea otter is a marine mammal often found along the coastal waters of the northern Pacific. It lacks the blubber of other marine mammals, like whales or seals. But it stays warm in icy waters thanks to its fur, which is the densest of any animal's. It also has unusually large lungs, two and a half times larger than those of similarly sized land mammals, and this helps it stay buoyant and dive efficiently. Sea otters have a loose flap of skin under each forearm that functions like a pocket, which they use for storing food and tools. That's right, tools. Sea otters are one of the few animals that use tools, using clam shells for digging and rocks to open clam shells. Now, wonderful as they may be, why care about sea otters? Because sea otters eat sea urchins, and sea urchins eat kelp. Kelp is a large seaweed that can grow up to thirty meters tall, providing food and shelter for a range of marine life. Kelp forests support a greater variety of marine life than almost any other ocean habitat. Kelp also grows very densely and acts as a natural buffer, protecting shorelines from storm damage. Finally, kelp forests are like rain forests in that they absorb and store carbon dioxide through photosynthesis, mitigating climate change and reducing ocean acidification.

### Listen to a Talk in an anthropology class – Questions 8-11

audio: listening-m2talk1.m4a
image: 26.png

Professor: Previously, we discussed the significance of pottery to ancient cultures, including pottery as an art form and pottery's crucial role in trade. Today, we'll focus on the development of pottery over time. One breakthrough was the invention of the potter's wheel around 3500 B.C.E. in Mesopotamia. Before this, pottery was shaped mostly by hand, which was slow and produced less uniform results. The potter's wheel allowed artisans to spin and shape the clay more quickly and evenly, making it possible to create symmetrical vessels with greater precision and speed. Firing technology also advanced. Early pots were fired in open pits, which limited temperature control, resulting in uneven or fragile pots. So kilns were developed, enclosed ovens that could reach higher and more consistent temperatures. These allowed for stronger, more durable pottery. Another advance was the updraft kiln, which directed heat upwards. By adjusting the airflow, potters could control how much oxygen reached the fire. This helped them produce pottery with specific colors, like red or black, depending on how the clay reacted to the firing atmosphere. Then, in ancient China, potters developed kilns that could reach very high temperatures. This allowed them to create stoneware and eventually porcelain. Porcelain is made from a special mix of fine clay and minerals, and must be fired at temperatures above twelve hundred degrees Celsius. The result is a smooth, white ceramic that is strong, lightweight, and slightly translucent. Porcelain was a major breakthrough because it was more durable and versatile than earlier pottery.

### Listen to a Talk in a literature class – Questions 12-15

image: 27.png

Professor: A common theme in literature is isolation, and that's what we'll explore today. Isolation can be a powerful motif, reflecting the psychological effects of being separated from society. One of the most classic examples of isolation in literature is Mary Shelley's Frankenstein. In this novel, Victor Frankenstein's grotesque creation, the creature, experiences profound isolation due to his monstrous appearance and the fear he instills in people. This isolation leads to anger and a desire for revenge, thus highlighting the detrimental effects of being ostracized. Another notable example is The Catcher in the Rye by J.D. Salinger. The protagonist, Holden Caulfield, feels disconnected from the world around him, and he struggles with alienation and loneliness. This social isolation is what drives Holden's actions and internal conflicts throughout the book. Yet another example of isolation in literature, and one that ends badly, is Herman Melville's Moby Dick. In this novel, Captain Ahab's obsessive, vengeful quest for the white whale leads to his isolation. This obsession distances him from his crew as well as his humanity, and it propels him and his ship toward a destructive end. However, the portrayal of isolation in literature is not always negative. In Charlotte Bronte's Jane Eyre, the titular character experiences isolation at various points in her life, such as when she is sent to live with her unkind relatives, and later when she experiences loneliness at a boarding school. But Jane is resilient in the face of isolation, becoming strong and independent.
