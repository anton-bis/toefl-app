subtitle: Flying Squid

image: 20.png

There are many species out there that we wouldn't expect to see flying. Mammals, reptiles, one fascinating creature is the flying squid. Now, squid have fins, arms, tentacles, and gills for breathing. Their main body, the mantle, is basically a tube of muscle that water goes inside of while they swim. When this mantle clamps shut, it pumps the water out, which propels the squid through water quite quickly. It's like a little jetpack. And flying squid are able to propel themselves right up through the ocean's surface and into the air above. Another unexpected flying creature is the flying squirrel, which can simply glide, sometimes quickly and for long distances, between tree branches. However, unlike the flying squirrel, flying squid not only glide, but can actually propel themselves upward. And, well, a flying squid can accelerate as fast as a car, getting up to 100 kilometers per hour in about a second. Then the squid extends its tentacles and arms, and it has membranes between them that spread out and help the squid glide for several seconds, a bit like temporary wings. A squid in flight is likely to be fleeing from an underwater predator or a ship. There are some alternative theories that one is that it's a migration strategy since moving through the air takes less energy and time than moving through water.

subtitle: Led Technology
audio: listening_m2_q08_q11_lecture_led_technology.mp3
image: 26.png

Imagine a time when the only lights available were flickering candles or oil lamps. Now fast forward to today where life is illuminated by the glow of energy shunt LED lights. So what makes these tiny light emitting diodes so revolutionary? Well the first practical visible spectrum LED was developed in 1962. It was a breakthrough that altered the landscape of lighting technology and set us on a path toward reduced energy consumption. LEDs, that's short for light emitting diodes, are a type of solid state lighting. They use a semiconductor to convert electricity directly into light. Unlike traditional incandescent bulbs which waste a lot of energy as heat, LEDs are incredibly efficient. That's their biggest benefit. They use about 75% less energy than incandescent bulbs and last 25 times longer. So that lowers electricity bills and also significantly reduces the carbon footprint. But the impact of LEDs goes beyond energy savings. They've enabled new lighting designs that were previously unimaginable. That's because they're small size which allows LEDs to be integrated into flexible strips, smart bulbs and even clothing. So virtually endless possibilities in architecture, art and everyday consumer products. And they're still developing. With ongoing research we could see such intriguing possibilities as LEDs that can purify the air. Improve your mood through human centric lighting. Or even transmit data in a system known as Li-Fi. A kind of wireless communication using light from LEDs.

In essence, Poverty Point defies our assumptions about ancient societies in North America.
